A clean filter helps maintain airflow, but replacement timing depends on your home, filter type, pets, and how often the system runs. ## Set a filter-check routine for North Mississippi summers

Check the filter about once a month during hot, humid weather. Replace it when it looks coated with dust or lint, or follow the interval printed on its packaging. A filter’s suggested lifespan is a starting point—not a substitute for checking it during a season of frequent AC use.

Homes near busy roads, renovation work, or fields may collect dust faster. Pets and heavier indoor activity can also mean more frequent changes. Use the filter size and type recommended for your system; a denser filter isn’t automatically a better choice if your equipment isn’t designed for it.

**Quick checklist:**

1. Turn the system off before removing the filter.
2. Find the size printed on the old filter or in the equipment manual.
3. Check the airflow-direction arrow and point it toward the equipment when installing the new one.
4. Make sure the filter fits snugly, without gaps or bending.
5. Write the installation date on the frame and check again in about a month.

## Watch for signs beyond a dirty filter

A filter that loads up quickly may point to unusually dusty conditions or a maintenance issue. Pay attention if airflow seems weak at several supply vents, rooms cool unevenly, or the system runs longer than usual without reaching the thermostat setting. Ice on refrigerant lines or the indoor coil, unusual noises, or repeated shutdowns also deserve attention.

Turn off the system if you see ice, then contact an HVAC professional rather than trying to chip it away. A filter change may help when restricted airflow is the cause, but those symptoms can have other explanations.

## FAQ

### Should I change my filter more often in Tupelo’s humid summer?
Check it monthly during heavy use. Replace it sooner if it’s visibly dirty, and follow the filter maker’s guidance.

### Can a dirty filter cause weak airflow?
It can restrict airflow, but weak airflow may also come from other equipment or duct problems. If a clean, correctly fitted filter doesn’t help, arrange an inspection.
